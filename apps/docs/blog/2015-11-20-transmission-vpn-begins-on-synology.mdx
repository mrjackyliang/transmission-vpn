Transmission VPN 1.0.0 shipped on November 20, 2015, as a shell script for Synology NAS. It connects a VPN profile from DSM and binds Transmission to the VPN's address, so torrent traffic has one way out.

{/* truncate */}

## Why It Mattered

DSM could connect to a VPN, and the Synology Transmission package could download torrents, but nothing tied the two together. When the VPN dropped, Transmission kept running on the regular connection.

The script closed that gap with one Transmission setting: `bind-address-ipv4`.

## What the First Release Did

- **`start`** — Connects the DSM VPN profile and binds Transmission to the VPN interface's address.
- **`stop`** — Binds Transmission to `127.0.0.1`, so it can't reach the internet while the VPN is off.

## What Came Next

Within two days, version 1.0.1 added a `repair` command that compared the VPN interface's address with the public address and reconnected when they differed, keeping Transmission on `127.0.0.1` while the VPN was down. It also brought OpenVPN (`tun0`) alongside L2TP and PPTP (`ppp0`).

Over the next five years, the script grew to version 1.3.2 with retry settings, a port forwarding check, and support for each new Synology Transmission package.

## Where It Led

The same idea — torrent traffic goes through the VPN or nowhere — now runs as one Docker image with a fail-closed firewall, OpenVPN and WireGuard, and a web interface. [Installation](/docs/getting-started/installation/) covers the current release, and the [Migration Guide](/docs/getting-started/migration/) helps if you ran the script.
